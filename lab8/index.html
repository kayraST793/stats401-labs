3. Semantic embedding map

Method: how the map and topics were produced

  • Embeddings. Each passage was encoded with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer into a 384 dimensional vector, L2 normalised.
  • Projection. UMAP reduced the vectors to two dimensions for the map (cosine metric, 15 neighbours, min_dist 0.15, random_state 401). Proximity is meaningful; the axes are not.
  • Clustering. k-means on the full 384 dimensional vectors, k = 8. A silhouette scan over 6, 8, 10, 12 gave similar low scores, and 8 produced the clearest topics.
  • Labels. Each cluster was named from its characteristic TF-IDF terms and the passages nearest its centre, then bound to distinctive terms so the naming is reproducible.

The interactive map

One point is one passage. Position is the UMAP embedding, colour is the semantic topic, and circle size is passage length in words. Click a passage for its details and to ring its five nearest neighbours. Use the search box, the topic filter, the section filter, and zoom or pan to focus on a region.

4. Topic by Section matrix

Rows are the 20 largest formal sections, columns are the eight semantic topics, and a darker cell holds more passages. Hover a cell for the exact section, topic, and count. The matrix shows which sections are focused on a single topic and which are diverse.

Coordinating the two views

The two views are linked. Click a matrix cell and the map filters to that section and topic. Click a point on the map and its cell is outlined in the matrix. This lets you move between where a passage sits in the bulletin and what it means.

5. Findings

Each pattern below was noticed in the two views and then measured against the underlying passages. The counts come from a text query over the 1100 passages, and can be checked in the map above by searching or filtering.

What are the major semantic topics?

Eight topics emerged. Four are course content (Politics, Society and Ethics 244; Arts, Humanities and Media 198; Quantitative and Computing Sciences 154; Natural and Life Sciences 72) and four are administrative policy (Transfer Credit and Study Away 128; Curriculum and Degree Structure 124; Academic Standing and Withdrawal 92; Advanced Placement and Grading 88).

Which topics appear across multiple formal sections?

The administrative topics are cross cutting. Transfer Credit and Study Away appears in 51 different sections, Academic Standing in 26, Advanced Placement in 23, and Curriculum in 19. The course content topics are concentrated in Course Descriptions and Majors (Natural Sciences in 2 sections, Arts in 4, Politics in 6, Quantitative in 7).

Which formal sections are most semantically diverse?

Course Descriptions spans all seven course and quantitative topics across 647 passages, and Majors spans six. After those, Degree Requirements, Grading and Grade Requirements, Undergraduate Course Load, Office of Global Education and Study Away, and Estimated Expenses each mix four topics.

Which passages from different sections are semantically similar?

The two Academic Warning, Probation, and Suspension sections (one for the classes of 2022-2024, one for the class of 2025 and beyond) hold near identical probation text (cosine about 0.99), the same policy duplicated for two cohorts. Grading and Grade Requirements sits close to Office of Global Education and Study Away (0.96) on study abroad credit, and Transfer of Work Taken Elsewhere matches Office of Global Education (0.92) on the 40 credit transfer cap. A keyword search across separate chapters would miss these links.

Where does the word "credit" occur across the bulletin?

"credit" appears in 159 passages spread across all eight topics, concentrated in Advanced Placement and Grading (65), Transfer Credit and Study Away (39), and Curriculum and Degree Structure (31). It is not one semantic region but a thread through several administrative topics. By contrast "academic integrity" appears in only 3 passages, so it is a short heading rather than a large theme. To see this in the map, type "credit" into the search box above: the highlighted passages spread across the right side administrative topics instead of forming a single cluster.

6. Design description

The corpus is the 2021-22 Duke Kunshan University Undergraduate Bulletin, a document of about 400 pages. I split it into paragraph sized passages and kept each passage's chapter, section, subsection, and page by reading the PDF's built in table of contents and locating every heading on its page. After cleaning, which repaired characters, merged paragraphs broken across page breaks, and dropped duplicated boilerplate, 1100 passages remained.

Each passage was encoded with the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence transformer into a 384 dimensional vector. I reduced those vectors to two dimensions with UMAP (cosine metric, 15 neighbours, minimum distance 0.15, seed 401) for the map, and clustered the full 384 dimensional vectors with k-means using eight clusters. Each cluster was labelled from its most characteristic TF-IDF terms and the passages closest to its centre. To avoid naming clusters by an arbitrary index, each label is tied to distinctive terms the cluster must contain, so a rerun relabels correctly or stops.

The map encodes position as the UMAP embedding, colour as the topic, and circle size as passage length. Clicking a passage shows its details and rings its five nearest neighbours, which often sit in different formal sections. Search, a topic filter, a section filter, and zoom help focus on a region. The second view is a Topic by Section matrix whose shading shows how many passages fall in each pairing. The two views are coordinated: clicking a matrix cell filters the map to that section and topic, and selecting a map point outlines its cell. This connects where a passage sits in the bulletin with what it means, which a single scatter plot cannot do.
