Lab 10: Novel Interaction with a Geospatial Visualization

This extends the Lab 9 geospatial visualization (the 2025 GDP choropleth and cartogram) with two novel input modalities. Voice highlights a country, and hand gestures seen through the webcam zoom and reset the map. The mouse still works exactly as before, so the novel inputs are added on top of the existing interaction, not in place of it.

Novel interaction

Voice: highlight a country

In the floating Controls panel (bottom-right), click Start Voice Control once and allow the microphone. It keeps listening, so you can say several commands in a row; click again to stop. Try:

  • "highlight Japan" → pin that country in both views
  • "select Germany" → pin that country in both views
  • "reset" → clear the selection and the zoom

Hand gesture: zoom and reset

In the floating Controls panel, click Start Camera, allow the webcam, then Start Gesture Detection. Then:

  • Pinch shape (index out, other fingers curled): thumb and index apart zooms out, together zooms in
  • Open palm → reset the view
  • Thumbs up → pause or resume

Zoom only responds in the pinch shape, so relaxing your hand holds the current zoom.

Controls panel. The voice and camera controls sit in a small panel pinned to the corner of the window, so they stay beside the map as you scroll and voice does not require scrolling back up. Click its header to collapse or expand it.

Fallback. The mouse controls from Lab 9 are unchanged: hover for a tooltip, click a country to pin it, scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and use the color-scale selector and Reset view button below. The visualization stays fully usable if the microphone or camera is unavailable or a command is not recognized.

1. Dataset

The GDP figures are the 50 largest economies by 2025 nominal GDP (current prices, billions of U.S. dollars) from the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025. Country boundaries come from a world GeoJSON keyed by ISO-3 code, which is the join key. Economies outside the top 50 are shown as missing data, not as zero.

2. 2025 GDP Choropleth

Color encodes 2025 GDP: darker means larger. Hover a country for its exact value and rank, scroll to zoom, and drag to pan. Voice and gesture act on this map.

Grey countries are outside the top-50 dataset (missing data, not zero). The legend and its tick spacing update with the selected scale.

3. 2025 GDP Cartogram

Each economy is a circle whose area is proportional to its 2025 GDP (radius scaled by the square root of GDP). Position is an approximate geographic layout; circles are pushed apart so none overlap. Hovering or selecting a country highlights it here too.

Area encodes GDP. True shape and exact position are deliberately sacrificed so that magnitude and rank can be read directly.

4. Design reflection

I extended the Lab 9 geospatial visualization, a 2025 GDP choropleth paired with a Dorling cartogram. I added two novel inputs: voice to highlight a country and hand gestures to zoom, reset, and pause the map. Voice supports the selection task: country names are discrete labels that speech recognition handles reliably, so saying "highlight Japan" pins that country in both views faster than hunting for a small shape with the cursor. Pinch gestures support navigation: the distance between thumb and index finger scales the map, an open palm resets it, and a thumbs up pauses tracking so the map stops following my hand. Each input is matched to a task it does well, discrete naming for selection and continuous hand distance for zoom, while the mouse remains for precise panning. The main limitations were recognition errors: speech sometimes misheard or returned nothing, and some table names differ from how people say them (Türkiye, Russian Federation), so I added spoken aliases. The gesture model also jittered the zoom and sometimes confused a relaxed hand, which smoothing, a cooldown, and the pause gesture reduced.

Submission checklist

  • Reuses the Lab 9 geospatial visualization.
  • Adds two novel interactions: voice selection and gesture zoom/reset.
  • Each interaction supports a specific task (highlight, zoom, reset).
  • Visible feedback: voice heard/action and gesture detected/zoom.
  • Conventional fallback kept: hover, click, scroll, drag, buttons.
  • Camera and microphone require an explicit button click.
  • Includes a design reflection.
