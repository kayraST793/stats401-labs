Lab 4: Tweet Sentiment Analysis

RoBERTa sentiment on a reproducible random sample of 1,500 ChatGPT tweets (Nov 2022 to Feb 2023). Source: deberain/ChatGPT-Tweets.
Note: the sentiment values are model-generated estimates produced by RoBERTa, not human-labeled ground truth, and carry the model's own error.

Sentiment over time (weekly)

Weekly tweet counts by sentiment (Monday-anchored). Each line shares a zero baseline, so the three sentiment trends are directly comparable. Hover a point for the exact count and its within-week share.

Takeaway: all three lines climb together toward an early-February 2023 peak as ChatGPT went mainstream, and every week neutral and positive sit well above negative - volume surged, but the mix stayed consistently favorable rather than turning hostile.

Average polarity over time (weekly)

The chart above counts tweets; this one measures mood. Each week is collapsed to the average sentiment score, where score = P(positive) - P(negative) per tweet, ranging from -1 (all negative) to +1 (all positive). The dashed line at 0 is net-neutral: points above it mean the average tweet leaned positive that week, below it negative. This answers a different question than the counts; not "how many tweets" but "which way did opinion tilt". Hover a point for the exact average and the week's tweet count.

Takeaway: every week stays net-positive (roughly +0.22 to +0.42, never crossing zero), peaking in the very first week and then easing into a steadier band. The earliest adopters were the most enthusiastic, and as the crowd broadened the mood cooled slightly but remained positive.

Sentiment by influence tier

Authors are bucketed by follower count into Micro (<1,000 followers), Mid (1,000 to 100,000), and Macro (>100,000). Bars show the share of each sentiment within a tier, so tiers of very different sizes stay comparable; read down a single colour across the three tiers to compare one sentiment. The n= label under each tier is its sample size. The steep drop (927 to 546 to 27) is expected, not a sampling flaw: follower counts are heavily right-skewed, so genuine large-audience influencers (Macro) are inherently rare, and a random sample should contain very few. Read Macro's shares as directional given its small sample. Hover for percentages and tweet counts.

Takeaway: the sentiment mix barely shifts from Micro to Macro, so reach does not meaningfully change opinion. Enthusiasm and skepticism are shared regardless of audience size (with Macro read cautiously at n=27).

Model confidence (how sure is RoBERTa?)

Sentiment labels are only as trustworthy as the model that produced them, so before reading anything into them it is worth asking how confident RoBERTa actually was. Each tweet's confidence is the probability it assigned to the winning class; a three-class model bottoms out near 0.33 (a near-tie between all three) and tops out at 1.00 (certain). The histogram below bins those confidences across all 1,500 tweets. The dashed line marks a 0.50 low-confidence threshold: bars to its left are tweets the model found genuinely hard (often sarcasm, mixed sentiment, or slang). Hover a bar for its count and share.

Takeaway: the distribution is heavily right-skewed with only 2.7% of tweets below 0.50, so RoBERTa is decisive on the vast majority. The sentiment labels are trustworthy enough to build the charts above on.

Analysis

This page applies a fine-tuned RoBERTa model (cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest) to a reproducible random sample of 1,500 ChatGPT tweets collected between late November 2022 and February 2023. Cleaning removed duplicate tweets and unparseable dates, filled missing locations, coerced follower counts to numeric, and lightly normalized text (HTML entities, whitespace, masked @users and URLs) before scoring. For each tweet the model returns three class probabilities; the label is the highest, and sentiment_score = P(positive) - P(negative). Four views build the picture. The weekly count and average-polarity lines show volume climbing to an early-February peak while mood stays net-positive every week (about +0.22 to +0.42), highest among the earliest adopters. Grouping authors into follower-based influence tiers, the sentiment mix barely moves. Micro, mid, and macro all sit near 12-15% negative, 45-48% neutral, and 37-41% positive, respectively, so the reach does not shape opinion. Finally, a confidence histogram shows only 2.7% of tweets below 0.50, so the model is decisive and the labels are trustworthy. Overall, early ChatGPT discourse was high-volume, broadly positive, and remarkably uniform across influence levels.
