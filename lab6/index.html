Lab 6: Hierarchical Data Visualization: Trees and Treemaps

Two treemaps of a synthetic GDP dataset. The hierarchy goes continent, then area, then country. Rectangle size shows the GDP in billions of dollars and rectangle colour shows whether GDP went up, stayed the same, or went down. The two treemaps use different tiling methods so the same numbers are laid out in two different ways.

Click a region to zoom in and reveal its countries, and click again to zoom back out.

Treemap 1: Squarify

Treemap 2: Binary

Design description

I put GDP on the size of each rectangle because size is easy to compare and area is what a treemap does best. The largest economies like the United States and China take most of the space, so the ranking is clear without reading numbers. GDP status goes on colour, green for an increase, grey for unchanged, and red for a decrease, which is a familiar reading and the legend explains it. Because colour is already used for status, I show the hierarchy with nested headers instead of a second colour, so each continent and area gets a labelled strip, and size, status, and grouping stay on separate channels.

Both treemaps hold the same hierarchy and values, so the only difference is how the space is cut. Squarify greedily keeps rectangles close to squares, which makes labels and areas easy to read and compare. Binary splits each group into two balanced halves by value, so it also produces compact rectangles but arranges them in a different order and shape. Comparing the two shows that the layout can change a lot while the sizes and colours stay the same.
