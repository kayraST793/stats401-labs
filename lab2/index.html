Lab 2: Multivariate Visualization with D3

This visualization explores the relationship among population, temperature, development level, and region.

Design Justification

Mapping:

  • population → x-position
  • temp_c → y-position
  • development_level → circle size
  • region → color

Rationale: Population is a ratio variable, so a linear x-axis allows differences in populations to be compared clearly. Temperature is an interval variable, so mapping it to the y-axis makes it easier to identify possible relationships between population and temperature. Development level is ordinal (Low, Medium, High), so circle size conveys rank order without implying exact numeric differences. Region is nominal, so different colors allow viewers to easily group and separate cities by area.

Viewer Goals: I hope viewers can use the visualization to notice patterns between population, temperature, development level, and region. The colors can help viewers identify whether cities from the same region cluster together based on their population and temperature. Viewers can also examine whether there is a positive, negative, or no clear relationship between population and temperature by looking at how the bubbles are distributed across the x- and y-axes. The different bubble sizes can help viewers see how development level varies across cities and whether it appears to be related to population or temperature. Finally, the tooltips allow viewers to examine individual cities more closely by providing their names and exact values, making it easier to compare specific observations.
