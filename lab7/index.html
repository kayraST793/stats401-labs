Lab 7: Animated Temporal Commercial Network

Twelve companies trade with one another over 60 days. The network below redraws itself as time moves, so you can watch relationships appear, disappear, and change strength.

Dataset

Each company is a node with a sector (Manufacturing, Logistics, Retail, Technology, Food, Wholesale, Materials) and a region (Asia, Europe, North America). Each transaction is an undirected link between two companies on a given day, with a dollar amount, a type (goods, shipping, components, materials, services), and a transaction count. The data run from 2026-01-01 (day 1) to 2026-03-01 (day 60) and are fabricated for visualization practice.

Design

Six channels carry the data so a single day can be read at a glance and the changes between days stay visible.

VariableChannelWhy it fits
Sector Node fill colour Sector is categorical. Colour is quick to scan and groups companies of the same kind without ranking them.
Region Node outline colour, plus horizontal band and a faint background tint Region is categorical. The outline colour leaves the fill free for sector, and a group-based force parks each region in its own vertical band (North America, Europe, Asia) with a faint matching tint behind it, so the grouping reads at a glance and cross-region links are easy to spot.
Current volume Node size (square-root) Volume is a quantity that changes each day. A square-root scale keeps node area proportional to the value, so busy companies grow honestly.
Trading today Node outline weight A thicker outline marks the companies active on the current day, which separates them from idle nodes that stay in place.
Amount Link width Amount is a quantity, and a thicker line reads as a larger trade.
Transaction type Link colour Type is categorical, so a colour separates the five kinds of trade without implying an order.

Two ideas keep the animation readable:

  • The twelve node objects are created once and reused every day, and the simulation is only reheated gently, so a company stays in roughly the same place from one day to the next. A group-based force also anchors each company toward a vertical band for its region, ordered west to east (North America, Europe, Asia) with a faint tint behind each band, so the three regions hold their own zones and the layout keeps its shape as time moves. Together these preserve the mental map.
  • A new relationship flashes to full strength for a moment and then settles, while a finished one fades out, so the eye can catch which links appear and disappear rather than seeing the whole graph blink. A large day label on the chart keeps the current day prominent.

Network Questions & Findings

Each answer is read from the animation above, supported by the encodings and the daily summary panel.

1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?

The network gets busier over the two months. On a quiet day only three pairs trade; on a busy day there are ten. The daily average rises from about 5.5 links in the first half to 6.7 in the second, the number of companies trading on a given day grows from roughly 8 to 9 out of 12, and the total value moving each day climbs from about $84,000 to $129,000. So both the count of relationships and the money flowing through them trend upward as the period goes on.

2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?

Fusion Electronics (C06) is the steady hub. It trades on more days than anyone else and carries the largest total volume, so it is usually the biggest node on screen. The clearest risers are Ion Systems (C09) and Cedar Retail (C03): each roughly triples its volume between the first and second halves, growing from a small dot early on to one of the largest nodes late in the animation. Apex Textiles (C01) also picks up. A few companies stay quiet throughout, for example Keystone Materials (C11) and Lumen Distribution (C12), which appear only occasionally.

3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?

The network is never a single connected whole. Every one of the 60 days splits into two to four separate clusters, about three on average. A typical day shows one larger group of four or five companies trading together, plus a couple of isolated pairs off to the side and some idle nodes with no links at all. The fragmentation eases slightly as the period densifies (from about 2.9 clusters early to 2.7 late), but the graph still does not join into one component on any day.

4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?

Only 27 of the 66 possible pairs ever trade, and none of them are one-offs: every relationship that exists comes back on several days. So the day-to-day fading in and out is a fixed backbone of about two dozen relationships taking turns, not random new partners. Among the recurring pairs, the ones that grow most are Fusion Electronics and Granite Wholesale (C06-C07), whose average trade rises from about $15,000 to $25,000, and Ion Systems and Juniper Stores (C09-C10), rising from about $13,000 to $20,000. A few edge down slightly, such as Fusion Electronics and Harbor Shipping (C06-C08), but the overall drift is toward stronger trades.

5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?

It becomes more cross-regional. Two-thirds of all trades already cross a region boundary, and that share rises from about 0.59 in the first half to 0.69 in the second. Trade within a single region is the minority. The busiest corridor by far is Asia to Europe, followed by Asia to North America and Europe to North America. So over the 60 days the companies increasingly do business outside their own region rather than close to home.
